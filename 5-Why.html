🎯 What makes a strong Why?

A strong Why should directly explain the previous statement, be supported by evidence, and lead to a cause that can be tested or controlled.

✅ Verification checklist

Evidence
Do I have data, measurement, observation or record?
Direct causal link
Does this actually explain the previous statement?
Reproducible
Can the failure be reproduced by introducing this cause?
Counterfactual test
Does removing the cause remove the problem?
Specific
Is the cause specific enough to act on?

⚠️ Common traps

Avoid statements such as “probably”, “operator error”, “poor quality”, or “needs more training” unless they are supported by evidence.

Also remember: 5 is not a magic number. Stop when you reach a verified, controllable cause.
